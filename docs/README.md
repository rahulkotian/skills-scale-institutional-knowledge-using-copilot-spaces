# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme uses a structured lifecycle to move work from idea to delivery: initiation, planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement. The process begins by validating the business need, clarifying measurable outcomes, identifying stakeholders, and deciding whether a project should proceed into planning. Once approved, the team creates a prioritized backlog, defines milestones, estimates effort, identifies dependencies and risks, and documents the Definition of Done so delivery is aligned on scope, quality, and ownership.

The operating model depends on clear roles and strong communication. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, resource planning, and status reporting, while Product Managers define the customer problem, success metrics, and backlog priorities. Developers deliver increments and maintain code quality, QA/testing validates acceptance criteria and critical flows, and stakeholders provide direction, approvals, and feedback. OctoAcme emphasizes visible progress through project boards, daily standups, weekly status checks, and milestone reviews so blockers and cross-team dependencies are surfaced early and addressed quickly.

During execution, OctoAcme follows disciplined delivery workflows: small pull requests, issue-linked work, CI validation, testing, and security checks before merge. The team tracks progress using project boards and dashboards, and it treats quality as part of delivery rather than a final gate. Any risks or issues are recorded in a risk register, escalated through the appropriate path when needed, and monitored during team and PM syncs to keep the work moving with minimal surprises.

When work is ready for release, the team confirms acceptance criteria, verifies staging and smoke tests, prepares rollback and mitigation plans, and announces the release to stakeholders and support. After each sprint, release, or critical milestone, the team undertakes a retrospective to capture lessons learned, define action items, and turn improvement opportunities into follow-up work. This creates a continuous feedback loop that strengthens delivery discipline while keeping execution aligned to customer value and business outcomes.
